Install

  1. Open the latest release on GitHub.
  2. Download the .apk file.
  3. Allow installs from your browser or file manager when Android asks.
  4. Install. Updating over an existing build with the same key keeps your data.

Reads the SMS you already have

Debit and credit messages from your bank and UPI apps, parsed on the device into amount, merchant, bank and category.

Encrypted where it lives

An AES-256-GCM ledger on your phone. The key is held by the Android Keystore, not written into the database and not stored on a server.

Works with no signal

Opens, records and totals without a network connection. A budget should not depend on a server being awake.

Budgets that warn early

A monthly cycle, per-category limits and thresholds you set, with an alert before the money is gone, not after.

Insights you can check

Category breakdown, seven-day activity and month-over-month comparison, computed from your own rows.

Yours to take with you

Export the whole ledger as JSON at any time. Delete individual rows, or clear the database completely.

Cloud backup

Not offered yet. SpendSense does not back your ledger up for you. Export a JSON copy and keep it somewhere safe.

Stated plainly rather than left out

What it refuses to do

The short list matters more than the feature list. Every line here is checked against the app and its permissions.

No bank credentials. It never asks for your bank login, card number or UPI PIN, and cannot move money.

No analytics or crash SDKs. Nothing reports your usage or sends a stack trace to a vendor.

No advertising, no data sale. There is no ad network in the build and nothing to sell.

No cloud by default. Sync, AI parsing, the assistant and Gmail import are each off until you turn them on.

No subscription. Free, with no in-app purchase and no paywall to export your own history.

No financial advice. It records and totals. It is not an adviser and it will not tell you what to buy.
